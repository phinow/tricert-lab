# Verified facts: Alberta privacy law, CTAS, CIHI NACRS, Power BI (checked 18 October 2026)

## How writers must use this file (read first)
- Kestrel Health Network (fictional) is a public body and an HIA custodian. Patient health information falls under the Health Information Act (HIA). Non-health personal information such as staff records falls under the Protection of Privacy Act (POPA). Never mention FOIP as current law; it was replaced on June 11, 2025.
- Cite HIA section numbers only for: section 60.1 (breach notification), section 64 (privacy impact assessment), section 60 (safeguards), sections 57 and 58 (highest degree of anonymity, least amount of information). The Act was amended in June and July 2026, so say "the Health Information Act" and the duty; do not cite any other section numbers. Never cite Bill 11 section numbers.
- HIA breach test is "risk of harm" (notify the Commissioner, the Minister and the affected individuals). POPA uses "real risk of significant harm". Do not mix them up. Do not name the Minister's portfolio; say "the Minister responsible under the Act".
- The Information and Privacy Commissioner reviews and comments on a privacy impact assessment; it does not approve it. An HIA privacy impact assessment must be submitted before the practice or system is implemented.
- No Alberta statute bans storing health information outside Canada; the HIA requires safeguards for information stored or used outside Alberta. Any "keep data in Canada" rule at Kestrel is a Kestrel contract or policy requirement, and must be described that way.
- CTAS: use the five level names and time objectives (immediate, 15, 30, 60, 120 minutes) and say they are objectives, not care standards. Do not state fractile targets other than 95% for Level II and 80% for Level V. The 2016 revision benchmark (median 1 hour, 90th percentile 3 hours from triage to physician initial assessment) may be used.
- Kestrel's definitions (state them as Kestrel's): ED clock starts at arrival_datetime (registration, which in Kestrel's data is never after triage); time to PIA = pia_datetime minus arrival_datetime; ED length of stay = departure_datetime minus arrival_datetime; LWBS = a visit that left before physician initial assessment (disposition LWBS). These follow the CIHI approach; do not claim CIHI publishes a single LWBS definition.
- Power BI: teach classic time intelligence with a marked date table (contiguous dates); mention that calendar-based time intelligence exists and is in preview.

## 1. Health Information Act (HIA), RSA 2000 c H-5

### Scope and recent amendments
- The HIA came into force on April 25, 2001. Source: https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/hia/
- The HIA WAS amended in this period. The Health Statutes Amendment Act, 2025 (No. 2) (Bill 11) was tabled November 24, 2025, received royal assent December 11, 2025, and its HIA amendments came into force on June 22, 2026, "with further amendments in force on July 2, 2026" (OIPC page, marked October 2026). OIPC does not say what the July 2 part contains. Sources: https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/hia/ ; https://www.alberta.ca/supporting-a-world-class-health-care-system ; https://oipc.ab.ca/alberta-information-and-privacy-commissioner-raises-concerns-regarding-amendments-to-health-information-act-as-set-out-in-bill-11/
- A new Health Information (Ministerial) Regulation (Alta Reg 106/2026) now sits beside the Health Information Regulation (Alta Reg 70/2001) and the Alberta Electronic Health Record Regulation (Alta Reg 118/2010). Source: https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/hia/ (links to all three)
- What Bill 11 changed, as described by the Commissioner when the bill was introduced (OIPC letter, December 1, 2025): a new "sharing custodian" model for "shared health information" (definitions in s 1(1)(v.2)/(v.3), rules in new ss 56.203 to 56.2092, including logging in s 56.209 and a PIA before becoming a sharing custodian in s 56.204); a definition of "automated system" (s 1(1)(c.3)) and rules for putting identifying health information into automated systems, including AI (ss 22, 27(1.1), 27.1); rules for abandoned health records (s 60.2); a researcher complaint power (s 74(4)); and authority to add personal health numbers to driver's licences and ID cards. The Commissioner called the sharing custodian model "a new model not seen anywhere in Canada." Bill 11 did NOT add administrative monetary penalties or whistleblower anti-retaliation protection. Sources: https://oipc.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/OIPC-Letter-to-Ministers-of-PPHS-and-HSHS-regarding-Bill-11-December-1-2025-1.pdf ; https://oipc.ab.ca/alberta-information-and-privacy-commissioner-raises-concerns-regarding-amendments-to-health-information-act-as-set-out-in-bill-11/
- Section numbers in the bullet above are from the bill as introduced. They were not checked against the final consolidated Act. Source: same OIPC letter.
- The Government of Alberta says the amendments support "team-based care, governance for shared health information," and privacy and data-driven innovation. Source: https://www.alberta.ca/supporting-a-world-class-health-care-system

### Custodians and affiliates
- Custodians are listed in s 1(1)(f) of the HIA and designated under the HIA regulations. Source: https://www.alberta.ca/health-information-act
- Custodians include the health ministers and departments (Primary and Preventative Health Services; Hospital and Surgical Health Services; Mental Health and Addiction; Assisted Living and Social Services), provincial health agencies and entities under the Provincial Health Agencies Act, Covenant Health, operators of approved hospitals and continuing care homes, ambulance operators, licensed pharmacies, the Health Quality Council of Alberta, the Canadian Centre of Recovery Excellence, regulated members of 13 designated health profession colleges, and others designated by regulation. Source: https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/hia/
- An "affiliate" is defined in s 1(1)(a) and "includes an individual employed by a custodian." Source: https://www.alberta.ca/health-information-act
- Affiliates include employees, contractors, volunteers, students, agents, information managers and privileged providers. Affiliates must comply with the Act, its regulations and the custodian's privacy management program. The custodian stays responsible for its affiliates. Source: https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/hia/

### Privacy management program
- Every custodian must establish or adopt policies and procedures under a privacy management program (s 63). The OIPC can ask for a copy, due within 30 days. Source: Government of Alberta fact sheet "HIA Fact Sheet: Privacy Management Program" (2026), copy hosted at https://nurses.ab.ca/media/k1epecy5/hia-fact-sheet_privacy-management-program.pdf ; also https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/hia/

### Least amount and highest anonymity
- s 57 and s 58 set the limitation and need-to-know principles. Source: Government of Alberta PMP fact sheet (link above)
- s 57: custodians must collect, use or disclose health information with the highest degree of anonymity possible, and must first consider whether aggregate health information is adequate (s 57(2)). Source: OIPC Investigation Report H2014-IR-01, https://dev2.oipc.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/H2014-IR-01.pdf
- s 58(1): a custodian must, in addition to complying with s 57, "collect, use or disclose only the amount of health information that is essential to enable the custodian or the recipient of the information, as the case may be, to carry out the intended purpose." Source: OIPC Order H2014-02, https://dev2.oipc.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Order-H2014-02.pdf
- s 58(2): in deciding how much to disclose, a custodian must consider the individual's expressed wishes as an important factor. Source: OIPC Electronic Health Record Systems guidance, https://dev2.oipc.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Electronic-Health-Record-Systems-2016.pdf

### Duty to protect (safeguards)
- s 60(1): a custodian must take reasonable steps in accordance with the regulations to maintain administrative, technical and physical safeguards that (a) protect confidentiality and privacy, (b) protect the confidentiality of health information stored or used in a jurisdiction outside Alberta or disclosed to a person outside Alberta, (c) protect against reasonably anticipated threats to security or integrity, loss, and unauthorized use, disclosure, modification or access, and (d) otherwise ensure compliance by the custodian and its affiliates. s 60(2) requires measures addressing the risks of electronic health records and proper disposal. Source (statute quoted): OIPC Order H2014-02, https://dev2.oipc.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Order-H2014-02.pdf ; current GoA page also cites s 60: https://www.alberta.ca/health-information-act

### Mandatory breach notification
- Section 60.1. An affiliate must tell the custodian (s 60.1(1)). The custodian must notify the Commissioner "as soon as practicable" where "there is a risk of harm to an individual as a result of the loss or unauthorized access or disclosure" (s 60.1(2)), and must also notify the Minister and the affected individuals (s 60.1(3)). Notice to individuals is in writing. Sources: https://oipc.ab.ca/?p=2544 (OIPC breach reporting page, June 2025) ; https://oipc.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Breach-Reporting-2018.pdf
- The test is "risk of harm", which is a lower bar than the "real risk of significant harm" (RROSH) test in POPA and PIPA. Source: https://oipc.ab.ca/?p=2544
- The Minister to notify is now the Minister of Primary and Preventative Health Services (not "Minister of Health"). Source: https://www.alberta.ca/health-information-act (published 2026-10-08)
- Risk-of-harm factors are in s 8.1 of the Health Information Regulation. A custodian may withhold notice to an individual if notice could reasonably be expected to harm the individual's mental or physical health (s 60.1(5)) but must then tell the Commissioner. Failing to notify is an offence (s 107). Sources: https://www.alberta.ca/health-information-act ; https://oipc.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Breach-Reporting-2018.pdf
- In force: August 31, 2018. Source: https://oipc.ab.ca/hia-breach-reporting/ (OIPC news, May 24, 2018)

### Privacy impact assessments (PIAs)
- s 64: each custodian must prepare a PIA describing how a proposed administrative practice or information system relating to the collection, use and disclosure of individually identifying health information may affect privacy, and must submit it to the Commissioner for review and comment BEFORE implementing it. Changes to existing practices or systems also need one. Sources: https://oipc.ab.ca/privacy-impact-assessments ; https://oipc.ab.ca/resource/privacy-impact-assessments-frequently-asked-questions/
- Other PIA submission triggers are in ss 46(1)(b), 46(5)(a), 56.3(3)(b) and 70(2) and (3). Source: https://oipc.ab.ca/privacy-impact-assessments
- After the 2026 amendments, PIAs are also needed before becoming a sharing custodian, joining a common or integrated program or service, or performing data matching. Source: Government of Alberta "HIA Fact Sheet: Privacy Impact Assessments" (2026), copy hosted at https://nurses.ab.ca/media/c55fosip/hia-fact-sheet_privacy-impact-assessments.pdf
- The OIPC does not "approve" or "accept" PIAs. Since October 1, 2024 it reviews them and issues a closing letter with comments and recommendations. Source: https://oipc.ab.ca/resource/changes-to-privacy-impact-assessment-process-now-in-effect-as-of-october-1-2024/
- Under the amendments, information managers (for example EMR, AI scribe or virtual care vendors) can submit their information security policies to the OIPC for review and comment. Sources: https://www.alberta.ca/health-information-act (Commissioner may comment on "information security policies prepared, and required to be submitted to the Commissioner" by a prospective information manager); secondary summary at https://iapp.org/news/a/pias-shared-custody-ai-highlight-changes-to-albertas-health-information-act

### Alberta EHR (Netcare) logging and audit
- The Alberta Electronic Health Record Regulation (Alta Reg 118/2010) "sets the requirements for comprehensive access logs" and for "regular audits and investigations." Source: https://www.albertanetcare.ca/learningcentre/EHR-Regulation.htm (Government of Alberta)
- Logging requirements come from s 6 of that Regulation. Logs must capture: user ID; name of user or application; user's role or job function; date of access; time of access; actions (create, view, edit, delete); facility or organization or authorizing custodian; type or class of health information accessed; unique identifier and name of the individual whose record was accessed; and anything else the Minister requires. Source: Government of Alberta, Provincial Logging and Auditing Standard v2.0 (September 15, 2025), https://www.albertanetcare.ca/learningCentre/documents/Provincial-Logging-and-Auditing-Standardv2.pdf
- Access logs must be kept for 10 years from the date of use (HIA s 56.6(2), per the same Standard). The Standard leaves audit frequency to each custodian. Source: same Standard.
- Individuals can ask for copies of logs showing who accessed or shared their information through the Alberta EHR. Source: https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/hia/
- Bill 11 adds a separate logging duty for sharing custodians (s 56.209: identity, individual, date and time, and other details). Source: OIPC Bill 11 letter (link above).

## 2. FOIP replaced by ATIA and POPA

- FOIP was repealed and replaced. The Access to Information Act (ATIA) and the Protection of Privacy Act (POPA, SA 2024 c P-28.5) both came into force on June 11, 2025. ATIA replaced FOIP's access part; POPA replaced its privacy part. FOIP may still apply in limited transitional cases. Sources: https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/atia/ ; https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/popa/ ; https://oipc.ab.ca/?p=2544 ("repealed June 11, 2025 and replaced by POPA")
- POPA applies to public bodies (ministries, agencies, boards, commissions, school boards, post-secondaries, municipalities, police, and others). Provincial health agencies and subsidiary health corporations are public bodies. Sources: https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/popa/ ; https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/atia/

### POPA breach notification
- s 10(2): if an incident involves loss of, unauthorized access to, or unauthorized disclosure of personal information "where a reasonable person would consider that there exists a real risk of significant harm to an individual", the public body must notify, without unreasonable delay, the affected individual, the Commissioner and the Minister. Notice must meet prescribed requirements (s 10(3); Protection of Privacy (Ministerial) Regulation s 4). Sources: King's Printer POPA consolidation (current as of June 11, 2025), copy at https://mhchs.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Protection-of-Privacy-Act-POPA-June-2025.pdf ; https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/ti-popa-fact-sheet-privacy-incident-response.pdf
- The Minister is the Minister of Technology and Innovation, notified through an online form on alberta.ca. Below the RROSH threshold, notifying the Commissioner and Minister is not required. Sources: https://www.alberta.ca/report-a-privacy-incident-public-bodies ; https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/ti-popa-fact-sheet-privacy-incident-response.pdf
- RROSH factors are in s 4(1) and (2) of the Protection of Privacy (Ministerial) Regulation. Source: https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/ti-popa-fact-sheet-privacy-incident-response.pdf

### POPA PIAs, privacy management program and other duties
- s 26(1): a public body must prepare a PIA "in prescribed circumstances" and submit it to the Commissioner where the regulations require. The PIA must identify and review risks and set out mitigation (s 26(2)). The Commissioner can ask for any PIA, due within 30 business days (s 26(3)). The circumstances are in s 7 of the Protection of Privacy (Ministerial) Regulation. Sources: POPA consolidation (link above); https://oipc.ab.ca/popa/pia/guide/ ; https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/popa-protection-of-privacy-act-guide.pdf
- Unlike the HIA, POPA does not require every PIA to be submitted to the Commissioner before implementation. Only prescribed PIAs are submitted. Sources: https://oipc.ab.ca/privacy-impact-assessments ; https://oipc.ab.ca/?p=16473
- s 25: public bodies must have a privacy management program. Under s 25(5) this was not required until one year after the section came into force, so it has applied since June 11, 2026. Sources: POPA consolidation (link above); https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/popa/
- s 11 bans the sale of personal information. ss 17 to 24 cover data matching and non-personal data. Fines run up to $125,000 (individual) and $750,000 (organization) for Part 1 offences, and up to $200,000 and $1,000,000 for Part 3 offences. Source: https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/popa-fact-sheet-key-differences-between-popa-and-foip.pdf

### Which law applies
- POPA s 3(2): POPA "does not apply to health information, as defined in the Health Information Act, that is in the custody or under the control of a public body that is a custodian" under the HIA. Source: POPA consolidation (link above)
- So for a public body that is also an HIA custodian (such as a provincial health agency): patient health information falls under the HIA, and non-health personal information such as employee records falls under POPA (with access requests under ATIA). Private-sector organizations fall under PIPA. Sources: POPA s 3(2) (above); https://oipc.ab.ca/?p=17473 ; https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/atia/

## 3. Storing health information outside Canada

- No blanket ban was found. Neither the HIA material reviewed nor POPA prohibits storing personal or health information outside Canada. Source (POPA, no location rule found in the full text): POPA consolidation (link above)
- The HIA expressly expects storage or use outside Alberta to happen and regulates it. s 60(1)(b) requires safeguards that "protect the confidentiality of health information that is to be stored or used in a jurisdiction outside Alberta or that is to be disclosed by the custodian to a person in a jurisdiction outside Alberta." Source: OIPC Order H2014-02, https://dev2.oipc.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Order-H2014-02.pdf
- Health Information Regulation s 8(4) adds requirements when health information is stored or used outside Alberta. The OIPC has said custodians must have written agreements before disclosing to recipients outside Alberta. Sources: https://oipc.ab.ca/hia-reminders/ (OIPC, 2004); s 8(4) is cited at https://www.cpta.ab.ca/for-physiotherapists/resources/privacy/information-management-agreement-frequently-asked-questions/ (secondary source)
- POPA s 60(4) makes it an offence to knowingly disclose personal information, derived data or non-personal data in response to a subpoena, warrant or order from a court or body with no jurisdiction in Alberta. This controls foreign compelled disclosure, not where data is stored. Source: POPA consolidation (link above)
- Contract rules that keep data in Alberta (for example the Alberta Medical Association template IMA clause "No Health Information shall be permanently stored outside the province of Alberta") are contract terms, not statute. Source: https://www.albertadoctors.org/emr_resources/IMA_-_Generic_-_with_HIA_7.2_Requirements-2015.pdf

## 4. Canadian Triage and Acuity Scale (CTAS)

- CTAS is reviewed by the CTAS National Advisory Committee, a partnership of NENA and CAEP. The latest adult guideline revision is the 2016 revision (CJEM 2017;19(S2):S18 to S27). Sources: https://ctas-phctas.ca/?page_id=17 ; https://ctas-phctas.ca/?page_id=294
- The five levels and time-to-physician objectives in the CTAS Implementation Guidelines: Level I Resuscitation, "Time to physician IMMEDIATE"; Level II Emergent, "≤15 min"; Level III Urgent, "≤ 30 min"; Level IV Less Urgent (Semi urgent), "≤ 1 hour"; Level V Non Urgent, "≤ 2 hours". Source: http://ctas-phctas.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/ctased16_98.pdf
- The guidelines call these times "ideals (objectives) not established care standards." Source: same document.
- "Fractile response" means "the proportion of patient visits for a given triage level where the patients were seen within the CTAS time frame defined for that level." The primary text confirms 95% for Level II and 80% for Level V. Sources: http://ctas-phctas.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/ctased16_98.pdf ; http://ctas-phctas.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/paedctas.pdf
- The 2016 revision keeps the level 1 to 5 time targets "unchanged". It also says the 80 to 95% fractile targets from the 1999 guidelines "are not achievable by busy EDs" and sets an overall benchmark: "triage to physician initial assessment is a median of 1 hours and 90th percentile of 3 hours." Source: https://core-cms.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E2CB3E2063C54E11259313FA4FEAE495/S1481803517003657a.pdf/revisions-to-the-canadian-emergency-department-triage-and-acuity-scale-ctas-guidelines-2016.pdf
- Nurse reassessment intervals while waiting (unchanged from the original guidelines): I continuous nursing care; II every 15 min; III every 30 min; IV every 60 min; V every 120 min. Source: https://core-cms.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/155B244E13174807590DAEDCBA8154A1/S1481803500009428a.pdf/revisions-to-the-canadian-emergency-department-triage-and-acuity-scale-implementation-guidelines.pdf (CJEM 2004;6(6):421 to 7)

## 5. CIHI NACRS definitions

- Date/Time of Physician Initial Assessment (NACRS data element): "Date and time when patient was first assessed by a physician in the ED." Triage Date/Time: "Date and time when the patient is triaged in the ED." Disposition Date/Time: "Date and time the decision was made about the patient's disposition." Date/Time Patient Left ED: "Date and time the patient physically leaves the ED." Source: https://indicatorlibrary.cihi.ca/sites/default/files/document/nacrs-data-elements-2023-2024-en.pdf
- ED wait time for physician initial assessment (indicator): the time from "the earlier of triage date/time or registration date/time" to "the date/time of physician initial assessment", in hours, usually at the 90th and/or 50th percentile. It excludes scheduled visits and Visit Disposition 61 (leave post-registration) and 63 (left after triage). Source: https://www.cihi.ca/en/indicators/emergency-department-wait-time-for-physician-initial-assessment (updated September 2026)
- ED length of stay ("Total Time Spent in Emergency Department"): from "the earlier of triage date/time or registration date/time" to "the date/time when a patient leaves the emergency department (ED)." It covers unscheduled visits and excludes Visit Disposition 61. Source: https://www.cihi.ca/en/indicators/total-time-spent-in-emergency-department-hours-percentile
- Admitted patients: the stop time is "the date/time when a patient is admitted and leaves the emergency department (ED)." Source: https://www.cihi.ca/en/indicators/total-time-spent-in-emergency-department-for-admitted-patients-90-spent-less-in-hours (updated September 2026)
- Non-admitted patients: the stop time is "the date/time when a patient is discharged, transferred or leaves the emergency department (ED)" (patient-left-ED or disposition date/time). Source: https://www.cihi.ca/en/indicators/total-time-spent-in-emergency-department-for-non-admitted-patients-hours-percentile
- CIHI Quick Stats rule: for admitted-type dispositions use Patient Left ED Date/Time if known, otherwise Disposition Date/Time. For discharged-type dispositions use Disposition Date/Time if known, otherwise Patient Left ED Date/Time. Source: https://www.cihi.ca/sites/default/files/document/emergency-department-visits-meth-notes-en.pdf (December 8, 2022)
- Left without being seen: CIHI does not publish a single "LWBS" indicator definition in the sources checked. Its Quick Stats group "Not seen or left" as Visit Disposition 61 (leave post registration), 62 (leave post initial treatment), 63 (left after triage) and 64 (left after initial assessment), from 2018 to 2019 onward. The strictest "not seen by a physician" codes are 61 and 63, which the PIA indicator excludes. Sources: https://www.cihi.ca/sites/default/files/document/emergency-department-visits-meth-notes-en.pdf ; https://learning.cihi.ca/nts/nd/fresco/repository/EKP000018634.pdf (CIHI NACRS Data Collection Fundamentals, January 2023)
- Alternate level of care (ALC): "When a patient is occupying a bed in a facility and does not require the intensity of resources/services provided in that care setting", the patient must be designated ALC by "the most appropriate care team member" (for example a physician, long-term care assessor, patient care manager or discharge planner). ALC time starts at the date and time of designation documented in the chart. It ends at departure from the ALC setting or when care needs change so that ALC no longer applies. Source: https://www.cihi.ca/sites/default/files/document/acuteinpatientalc-definitionsandguidelines_en.pdf (CIHI, 2016)

## 6. Power BI time intelligence (Microsoft Learn)

- Calendar-based time intelligence is still labelled preview: "Calendar-based time intelligence (preview)". You must turn on the "Enhanced DAX Time Intelligence" preview feature, and Microsoft warns "Performance of this preview feature isn't representative of the end product." Microsoft still recommends it: "We recommend using calendar-based time intelligence (preview)..." Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/transform-model/desktop-time-intelligence (metadata updated 2026-10-07)
- Classic time intelligence still exists and is not deprecated. It "requires you to have a date table in your model and to set it accordingly", "only works with models that have at least one dedicated date table", and needs complete dates: "if there are any missing dates between the first and last dates, an error is thrown." Source: same page.
- Mark as date table: you "have to mark your date table if you use the Classic time intelligence functions" or if relationships to the date table use a non-date data type (for example integer keys like 20241231), or for advanced date filters in Excel PivotTables. Marking is not needed for calendar-based time intelligence "unless in specific circumstances." Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/transform-model/desktop-date-tables
- Marking validates that the column has unique values, no nulls, contiguous dates from start to end, and (for Date/Time) the same timestamp on every value. Source: same page.
- DAX functions such as DATESYTD now accept either a date column or a calendar reference. Week functions (for example DATESWTD) "only work with calendar based time intelligence." Sources: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dax/datesytd-function-dax ; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ca/dax/dateswtd-function-dax
